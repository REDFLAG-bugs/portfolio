02 · How I got here

A coverage problem, not a demo problem.

Most ASR systems are built on the speech that is easiest to collect: read prompts, high-resource languages, typical articulation. The work I care about sits outside that set.

I grew up around Odia. The distance between what people actually say — dialect, disorder, code-mixing — and what a model transcribes is not a curiosity. It is a coverage problem. At Squbix I learned to ship: Whisper fine-tunes for Hindi, Telugu, and Odia, streaming over WebSockets, Docker on EC2. At IISc I went deeper — multilingual dysarthric recognition, severity estimation for ALS and Parkinson’s, and language models trained on the Indian languages that still lack them.

JAGA Labs is the other thread. Odia has almost no systematic dialect coverage. I started a registry and a collection effort across Odisha’s thirty districts so the next models have something real to train on.

05 · Side project

Odia dialect corpus initiative

JAGA Labs

An open registry of Odia speech data and a collection effort across Odisha’s 30 districts. Existing public corpora (Vaani, MUCS 2021 / OpenSLR 103) cover only four districts and almost no spontaneous conversational speech. JAGA catalogues what exists, cleans what is contaminated, and coordinates native-speaker collection for the rest.

  • Script-level audit of the Vaani Odia subset: removed 21 utterances with Bengali, Assamese, or Devanagari contamination. Cleaned split released on Hugging Face (CC-BY-NC 4.0).
  • Vaani-OR extraction: ~52.5 hours across train/val/test, 55.6 GB, district-tagged spontaneous speech.
  • Native-speaker translation of Odia utterances into Hindi and English for a parallel low-resource corpus.
